Research/Pitch Framework

Pitch Framework

Every stock pitch on this site follows the same five steps, from the industry down to one value per share. The exhibits below are real, each taken from a pitch.

Step 1 of 5

Map the industry

Who sells what to whom, how big the market is, how fast it grows and where the profit sits. I want to know who a company competes with before I judge how good it is.

Step 2 of 5

Understand the business

Segments, customers and the few numbers that drive results. Most companies earn most of their profit in one place, and that place deserves most of the attention.

Step 3 of 5

Build the model

Revenue, margins, earnings and cash flow, forecast from the filings with every assumption written down, so a reader can disagree with one number instead of the whole pitch. Spin-offs, acquisitions and share counts get fixed here, before any multiple touches the earnings.

Step 4 of 5

Value it three ways

A discounted cash flow valuation asks what the business is worth on its own cash flows. Trading comps and precedent deals ask how the market prices similar businesses. I set all three against the 52-week range and analyst targets, and when they disagree, I explain which one I trust and why.

Step 5 of 5

Turn the risks into scenarios

Each risk becomes part of a bear, base or bull case with a probability and a value. For a young company, I add a separate chance that the business fails.

The weighted value, set against the price, gives the rating: Buy when my value is at least 15% above the price, Sell when it is at least 15% below, and Hold in between. For a young company that could fail, the band widens to 25%, because the value itself is less certain. Every pitch ends with the prices at which its rating would change and what would change my mind.

The cost of capital behind every valuation is the risk-free rate plus beta times the equity risk premium, updated when markets move. I explain the latest change in The price of risk, not the price of money.
