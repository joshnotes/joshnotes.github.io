# How this site is written

The model is Aswath Damodaran's blog, Musings on Markets: a teacher thinking out loud, who tells you the story, turns it into numbers, values it, and shows his work so you can disagree with him. Everything on this site, Thoughts and Research, is written that way. The two sections differ in length and depth, not in voice.

## The seven habits

1. **Start with the story and why it matters now.** Open in the first person with what happened and why you're writing about it. Then say what the piece will do, and, if it helps, what it won't.
2. **Teach before you use.** If the argument rests on a concept (cost of equity, backlog, a Reverse Morris Trust), explain it in plain words the first time it appears. Assume a smart reader who isn't in finance.
3. **Story, then numbers, then value.** Every claim gets a number, and every number gets a date and a source. The conclusion should follow from the numbers, not decorate them.
4. **Put the assumptions on the table.** Name the inputs that drive the answer and show what happens when they change. Give readers the levers: "if you believe X, the value is Y."
5. **Keep price and value apart.** What the market pays (multiples, comparables, momentum) is not the same as what the business is worth (cash flows, growth, risk). Say which one you're talking about.
6. **Be honest about uncertainty, and about changing your mind.** When new facts break an earlier view, say so, date the revision and explain what changed. Being wrong in public, and fixing it, is part of the job.
7. **End with an action and a trigger.** What would I do, at what price, and what would make me change my mind?

## Sentences

- Write in full paragraphs that carry an argument. Mix long and short sentences. Avoid one-line paragraphs used for drama.
- Use plain words. Prefer "I think" to "it is clear that", and use hedges only where the doubt is real.
- No slogans, no hype words ("game-changer", "massive", "unlock"), no reflexive "It's not X, it's Y", no lists of three for rhythm, no "Here's the thing", no "In conclusion".
- Humor is fine when it's dry and about the subject, never about the reader.
- Numbers: $8.9 billion in prose, $8.9B in exhibits; percentages to one decimal only when it matters; dates as "November 20, 2026" in prose.

## Structure

- **Thoughts:** 900–1,400 words. One idea, one concept taught, one exhibit or two, a clear take, and what would change my mind.
- **Stock pitches:** follow the five steps on [Pitch Framework](/research/method/): map the industry, understand the business, build the model, value it three ways (trading comparables, precedent transactions, DCF) and cross-check against the price, then write the risks as bear, base and bull cases with probabilities.
- **Exhibits:** numbered ("Exhibit 1"), with a title that states the finding, the chart or table, and a "Source:" line. Mark estimates as mine.
- **Revisions:** a dated note at the top of the piece when the view changes.
- **Sources:** a numbered list at the end, primary documents (SEC filings, company releases) first.
