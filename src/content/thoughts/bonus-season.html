Johnson Associates' latest forecast has Wall Street bonuses rising for a third straight year, with increases in 20 of the 21 job categories it tracks. Equity traders and underwriters lead, with jumps of 20% to 30% or more.

The increases follow a strong year for the businesses that pay them. Global investment banking fees reached $99.4 billion in the first nine months of the year, up 9% and the best since 2021. First-half M&A set a record at about $2.8 trillion of announced deals, nearly half of it in megadeals above $10 billion.

Bonuses pay for last year's deals

A bonus pays for deals already done. The question for 2027 is what gets done next, and the backdrop changed fast: the 10-year Treasury spent October and most of November above 5% before falling to about 4.9% this week, the Fed is leaning toward a December hike, and the VIX touched 30 this week for the first time since late March.

Higher rates hit some deals harder than others. Strategic megadeals, often paid in stock and driven by boardroom conviction, can ride through them. Leveraged buyouts have a harder time, because when debt costs more, private equity sponsors either pay less or stop bidding, and the private credit funds that financed so many of those deals have problems of their own.

My take

This year's bonuses are backed by fees that have already been earned, so I would not worry about them. Next year's are a different matter. If fourth-quarter deal announcements hold near the first-half pace with yields near 5%, the deal cycle can carry into 2027. If they fall off, 2026 will look like the peak, and at least one pay consultant is already saying it might be.

What would change my mind

Fourth-quarter announcements matching the first half, and leveraged loan issuance holding up with Treasury yields near 5%.
