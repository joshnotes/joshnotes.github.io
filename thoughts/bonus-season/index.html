Wall Street is about to get paid for a market that just changed

Johnson Associates expects bonuses to rise for a third straight year, led by equity traders and underwriters. The harder question is whether the deals that paid for them keep coming at today's rates.

Joshuva JohnsonNov 21, 2026

Johnson Associates' latest forecast has Wall Street bonuses rising for a third straight year, with increases in 20 of the 21 job categories it tracks. Equity traders and underwriters lead, with jumps of 20% to 30% or more.

It's earned. Global investment banking fees reached $99.4 billion in the first nine months of the year, up 9% and the best since 2021. First-half M&A set a record at about $2.8 trillion of announced deals, nearly half of it in megadeals above $10 billion.

Bonuses look backward. Pipelines look forward.

A bonus pays for deals already done. The question for 2027 is what gets done next, and the backdrop changed fast: the 10-year Treasury spent October and most of November above 5% before falling to about 4.9% this week, the Fed is leaning toward a December hike, and the VIX touched 30 this week for the first time since late March.

Not every deal reacts the same way. Strategic megadeals, often paid in stock and driven by boardroom conviction, can ride through higher rates. Leveraged buyouts can't. When debt costs more, sponsors pay less or don't bid. And the private credit funds that financed so many of those deals have problems of their own.

My take

This year's pay is real. The people who should worry are the ones counting on next year's. If fourth-quarter deal announcements hold near the first-half pace with yields near 5%, the cycle has legs. If they don't, 2026 will look like the peak, and at least one pay consultant is already saying it might be.

What would change my mind

Fourth-quarter announcements matching the first half, and leveraged loan issuance holding up with Treasury yields near 5%.
